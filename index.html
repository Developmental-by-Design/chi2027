Developmental by Design

Bridging developmental science and the design of AI

CHI 2027, Pittsburgh, PA, USA Conference: May 10–14, 2027 Format: in person, two sessions

Abstract

A preschooler in a classroom with supportive teachers plays with an AI teddy bear that always has an answer. The preschooler quietly loses chances to build relationships and practice social-emotional skills. Across the world, a child walks alone into a refugee camp, unable to speak the local language. The same bear speaks their language and helps them connect with adults and practice social-emotional skills. One tool, in two contexts, has different developmental impacts on children of the same age. How do we account for that complexity and risk when designing AI for learning across the lifespan? Research largely measures AI’s task efficacy and users’ perceptions; its developmental impact remains underexplored. This workshop engages with the Developmental by Design framework, a map and six design practices from developmental and learning science. It is for people who build, research, or teach with AI that reaches learners; each participant brings one real project.

Three goals for the workshop

  1. 1

    Bring a developmental lens to HCI

    Connect developmental and learning science to the design and evaluation of AI, using the Developmental by Design map and six design practices to critique real projects.

  2. 2

    Build a shared vocabulary

    Build and test a shared vocabulary for judging the developmental impact of generative AI, so design critique can go further than "be careful with AI."

  3. 3

    Set a research agenda

    Leave with a research agenda the room has written, built from the big questions participants bring.

Call for participation

The workshop

This in-person workshop runs as two back-to-back sessions of 75–90 minutes. We introduce Developmental by Design, a two-axis map and six design practices from developmental and learning science. Participants evaluate a real system with it, use it to define their audiences and developmental objectives, then critique each other's projects in small groups.

Who should join

Designers, engineers, researchers, educators, and policy leaders working on AI that reaches learners, from early childhood to the workplace. All career stages are welcome. No developmental science background is needed, and work in progress is encouraged.

What to submit

A 2–4 page position paper on one project, product, lesson, intervention, or policy, in the single-column ACM template. Papers are not anonymized.

Attendance and publishing

We expect 20–40 participants. Authors of accepted papers are prioritized for places; remaining seats are open to any registered CHI attendee. One author of each accepted paper must register and attend. Papers are non-archival and will be posted on this website; a synthesis will follow on arXiv.

Organizers

Both organizers work at the Compass Collaborative at the Harvard Graduate School of Education, where Developmental by Design was developed and taught.

Tessa Forshaw

Faculty Chair, Workforce and Workplace Learning; Co-Director, Compass Collaborative, Harvard Graduate School of Education

A cognitive scientist, Tessa studies how people develop the cognitive and creative skills to learn, work and create alongside AI, and how emerging technologies shape learning and development. She was previously a Lecturer at Stanford's d.school and spent more than a decade at Accenture, the IDEO CoLab and People Rocket. She is the author of Innovation-ish (Wiley, 2025) and holds a Ph.D. from Harvard University.

Beth Sapire

Senior AI and Human Development Research Associate, Compass Collaborative, Harvard Graduate School of Education

Beth's research examines how generative AI can support and hinder development in adult learners. She leads the development of a chatbot that helps educators draw on developmental and AI research when deciding how to use AI. She facilitates executive education courses and was previously a consulting leader in Accenture's Talent and Organization practice.
